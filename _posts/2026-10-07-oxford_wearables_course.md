Recently, I attended the "Machine Learning of Wearables in Large Scale Biomedical Studies" course at the University of Oxford hosted by the Oxford Wearables Group.

Through engaging lectures and practicals, we learned how to leverage machine learning to unlock novel health insights. During the course, a few things became clear:

⌚ Wearable devices are amazing! Wearables capture activity, heart measures, sleep, and more at high resolutions. Self-reported measures are prone to biases and intermittent doctor visits can only capture so much.

📈 The importance of model validation and harmonization across devices, wear locations, and processing algorithms.

🌐 Open-source models, data sharing, and methods transparency are key for scientific progression.

‼️ The urgent need for more data from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), as well as accurate models for these countries. Models developed for high-income country data do not always generalize well to LMICs.

It was wonderful to learn from the Oxford Wearables Group, physician-scientists using wearables for health monitoring, industry researchers, and Fiona Bull from WHO. I also really enjoyed meeting and learning from ~30 classmates from 13(!) countries with diverse research backgrounds.

In 2025, the UK Government included wearables in its '5 Big Bets' for the future of healthcare. The future of wearables for health is bright! ✨ I am super inspired and excited to continue research in this field.

Thank you to my advisor, David Raichlen, as well as USC Dornsife PhD Academy and the Davis Graduate Research Award for making this opportunity possible!
