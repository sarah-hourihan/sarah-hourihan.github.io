Spring semester and summer of PhD Year 2 have officially wrapped up. I always add this, but time has truly gone by so fast. While individual days can feel minuscule, it is encouraging to look back at the last several months and realize the progress that was made. Below you’ll find:

* **Five** areas of growth
* **Four** highlights outside of lab
* **Three** moments of connection
* **Two** important tasks completed
* **One** goal for the future

### Five areas of growth:
1. Shifting my mindset to view the PhD process as both a learning _and_ research experience. This is obvious on the surface, but it is easy to get lost in the productively culture of aspiring for endless achievements. It takes intentional effort to accept the time it takes to learn new knowledge and skills. Despite my title being PhD **student**, I often forget that I am in training. Learning takes time. I will make mistakes along the way. While I want to maintain high expectations for myself, I am also trying to embrace this season of (sometimes uncomfortable) growth.
2. Networking at conferences, with collaborators, and at industry events. Over the past several months, it has been amazing to connect with researchers in these spaces. As an introverted person, it can be easier to keep to myself. However, I always leave these interactions feeling excited about the new connection and our shared passion for science.
3. Expanding my teaching capabilities through the [Future Faculty Teaching Institute](https://cet.usc.edu/future-faculty-teaching-institute/) at the USC Center for Excellence in Teaching. For 12 weeks, I attended interactive sessions that covered course design for inclusive teaching, facilitating conversations, and techniques for active learning. I look forward to applying what I learned in the classroom.
4. Learning new skills and analyses. It has been exciting to learn new analyses, build new models, and find new ways to approach research questions.
5. Undertaking my first review project! In February, I began a scoping review project. Since this is my first, it has been a time of immense growth. I have learned how to systematically search databases, how to screen thousands of articles, how to lead a team 8+ reviewers, how to extract consistent data, and how to map results. 

### Four highlights outside of lab:
With my qualifying exam and funding applications looming, it is critical for me to have outlets outside of research. For not burning out, for feeling grounded, for being refreshed when I _am_ doing research. All of the above!
1. Trying new restaurants and cafes around LA. I have become quite the foodie since moving here. Some of my favorite discoveries from this spring and summer include ramen from Mensho, matcha from rōk, pad wood sen from Civilize Thai Bistro, jerk chicken from Country Style Jamaican, and anything from anko cafe. 
2. Spending time in nature. California has lovely weather, beautiful views, and diverse ecosystems. Highlights were Joshua Tree National Park, Channel Islands National Park, and La Jolla :)
3. Playing in the USC student orchestra. I am grateful to experience the collective joy of making music, even while in grad school. 
4. Reading. Favorites from this year so far include _Demon Copperhead_ by Barbara Kingsolver, _Everything is Tuberculosis_ by John Green, _Anxious People_ by Fredrik Backman, and _Dark Matter_ by Blake Crouch.

### Three moments of connection:
1. Mentoring five undergraduate students. At the beginning of the spring semester, I began working with five undergrad research assistants. It is a privilege to guide them on their research journeys. It has been so rewarding to witness them achieve goals and share research findings in public spaces. So far, they have presented at the USC Undergraduate Research Symposium, gotten acceptances to present at an international conference, and secured their own research fellowships. I am so proud of them!
2. Attending the American Association of Biological Anthropologists (AABA) conference in Denver, Colorado in March. It was amazing to connect with other scientists in the fields of anthropology and human biology. The community is one of my favorite parts of being in academia :)
3. Volunteering as a guest scientist at a local elementary school’s science fair. The students were very excited to share their projects and ask us questions. I am constantly in awe of how curious young children are, and the way they pursue answers without insecurity.

### Two important tasks completed:
1. Wrapping up analyses for two (?) projects. The question mark is there because there might be additionally analyses. But they are mostly wrapped up! I am starting to see the light at the end of the tunnel. Even if it is just a single photon of light, it’s still there.
2. Applying to an exciting training program at the University of Oxford.

### One area of growth for the future: 
1. Setting ~strict~ deadlines for myself. I kinda thrive off of deadlines! In my head, I always have a loose timeline of when to complete tasks and projects. However, setting stricter deadlines will keep myself accountable and more productive. Executing this includes assigning specific dates for completion, being clear with my goals, physically writing down deadlines to make them concrete, outlining smaller sub-steps, and not putting off difficult tasks with 'easier' ones.

My PhD experience has stretched and pulled me in many directions. Yes, it is difficult, but it is also so rewarding and a privilege to be able to learn in this setting. I am grateful and look forward to what is to come!
